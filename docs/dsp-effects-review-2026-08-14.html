Ardor DSP Effects Review

Modulation, delay and reverb blocks — hosted daisyfx engine.
Date: 2026-08-14 · Target: Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (Cortex-A72, 1.5 GHz) · Scope: 29 effect modes plus the shared DSP primitives they use.
Constraint applied: every recommendation must fit alongside a full-tier NAM model and a 4096-tap cabinet IR at 32 frames.
Status: all 32 findings implemented, 31/31 tests passing. See the implementation status section below.

Summary

The delay bank is in good shape. The 2026-08-13 delay review was acted on: DelayLineSdram gained a 16-tap, 256-phase band-limited interpolator (ReadAtHighQuality), and every delay mode now selects between an integer tap for static delays and the band-limited tap for moving delays.

That fix never reached the other two families. The reverb tanks and every modulation mode still read moving taps with linear or cubic interpolation. In a feedback structure this loss compounds on every pass, and it is the single largest quality limiter in the project. The measured numbers are in finding P0-1.

Beyond that, three structural items hold the effects below studio standard: one hardcoded allpass delay table shared by every reverb and both channels, a broken crossover in Rotary, and a total absence of oversampling around the saturation stages.

CPU is not the obstacle. The measured reverb cost is 2.9–4.0 % of the callback budget and the delays are under 3.5 %. The highest-value fix in this document (P0-1) is cheaper than the code it replaces.

Method

Every mode file and every shared primitive was read in full. Two claims that drive the top findings were verified numerically rather than asserted: the frequency response of each interpolation kernel at its worst-case fractional offset, and the summed response of the Rotary crossover. Those results appear inline below. All other findings are read from the source and are labelled with the file and line.

CPU figures marked measured come from benchmark-results/ (2026-08-13, Pi 4, performance governor, isolated runs). Figures marked estimated are instruction counts scaled to 1.5 GHz and should be confirmed on the device before you commit to them.

P0 — Fix first

P0-1The reverb FDN reads modulated taps with linear interpolation

src/daisyfx/hosted/dsp/fdn.cpp:159 — affects Room, Hall, Cloud, Shimmer, Bloom, Chorale, Nonlinear and Swell (8 of 12 reverbs)

Fdn::readModulatedDelays() calls lines_[i].ReadLinear(modulated_delay_[i]) whenever modulation is active. Two-point linear interpolation has a severe magnitude droop, the tank runs at 24 kHz so every audio frequency sits proportionally closer to Nyquist, and the tap is inside the feedback loop, so the loss is applied again on every circulation.

Measured magnitude error at the worst-case half-sample offset:

Frequency Linear (current) Hermite 16-tap sinc
1 kHz−0.075 dB−0.001 dB0.000 dB
2 kHz−0.301 dB−0.015 dB0.001 dB
5 kHz−2.011 dB−0.534 dB0.001 dB
8 kHz−6.021 dB−3.255 dB−0.017 dB
10 kHz−11.740 dB−8.414 dB−1.674 dB

Per single read, tank rate 24 kHz, fractional offset 0.5 samples.

A 2 s reverb tail with a 60 ms mean loop circulates roughly 30 times. Compounded over 30 passes:

FrequencyLinear × 30Hermite × 3016-tap sinc × 30
2 kHz−9.0 dB−0.4 dB0.0 dB
5 kHz−60.3 dB−16.0 dB0.0 dB
8 kHz−180.6 dB−97.6 dB−0.5 dB

At 5 kHz the tail loses 60 dB purely to interpolation. This is why the modes read dark and why the Mod knob makes them darker: SetModulation() is what forces the fractional read path in the first place. With modulation at zero the code takes the integer fast path and the reverb is bright; turn Mod up and the tank loses its top end. That coupling is not a design choice, it is an artefact.

Recommended fix: a first-order allpass fractional delay, not a longer FIR.

The FDN base delays are integers and modulation is capped at ±8 samples at ≤ 2.52 Hz (fdn.cpp:10-12, MAX_MOD_DEPTH_SAMPLES). That is exactly the regime a first-order allpass interpolator is built for. Its magnitude response is exactly unity at every frequency by construction, so cumulative loss over any number of feedback passes is zero. It costs one multiply and two adds — less than the two reads and one multiply that ReadLinear already spends. 

// H(z) = (a + z^-1) / (1 + a*z^-1),  a = (1 - d) / (1 + d),  d = fractional part
// Magnitude is unity for all |a| < 1. Only phase (group delay) is approximated.

Keep d in roughly [0.1, 1.1] by folding the integer part, which is where the first-order allpass group delay is most accurate. Reuse the existing AllpassFilter in dsp/allpass_filter.h — it is already this exact transfer function.

If you prefer not to introduce state per tap, ReadAtHighQuality is the drop-in alternative: 8 lines × 16 taps = 128 multiply-accumulates per tank sample at 24 kHz.

Cost — allpass: negative (removes one read per line) Cost — sinc16: estimated +0.8 to 1.2 % of one core; reverb rises from ~3.4 % to ~4.4 % of budget

P0-2The Rotary crossover cancels a full octave around its crossover point

src/daisyfx/hosted/modes/rotary_mode.cpp:118-120

Rotary splits the signal into a drum band and a horn band by taking the low-pass and high-pass outputs of one TPT state-variable filter: 

xover_.Process(mono);
const float drum_in = xover_.lp();
const float horn_in = xover_.hp();

An SVF satisfies the identity HP + k·BP + LP = input. Therefore LP + HP = input − k·BP. With SetQ(0.5f) the damping term is k = 1/Q = 2, and at the crossover frequency the bandpass magnitude is exactly Q = 0.5 and in phase with the input. The two bands sum to zero.

Measured, by impulse response of lp() + hp(), Q = 0.5, fc = 800 Hz:

FrequencySummed response
200 Hz−1.09 dB
400 Hz−4.43 dB
600 Hz−11.07 dB
800 Hz−72.23 dB
1000 Hz−13.13 dB
1600 Hz−4.41 dB
3200 Hz−1.06 dB

The Tone knob sweeps this null from 500 Hz to 2000 Hz (rotary_mode.cpp:96) — directly across the fundamental range of a guitar. The two bands receive different delays and different amplitude modulation afterwards, so the null is partly masked in motion, but the hole is present in the source material before any of that happens.

Fix. Make the split complementary. The cheapest correct form is to derive one band by subtraction:

xover_.Process(mono);
const float drum_in = xover_.lp();
const float horn_in = mono - drum_in;   // sums to unity, magnitude and phase

That is exact reconstruction at zero extra cost. If you want a steeper, textbook Leslie crossover, use Linkwitz-Riley 4th order: two cascaded Butterworth (Q = 0.707) SVFs per band with the high band inverted, which sums to allpass. That costs one more SVF per band.

Cost: zero (subtraction) or estimated +0.15 % (LR4)

P0-3Magneto reads all tape heads with linear interpolation inside its feedback loop

src/daisyfx/hosted/modes/magneto_reverb.cpp:113-114

Head positions are computed as period * (i+1) / n_heads_, so they are almost never integers, and every one is read with ReadLinear. Those same taps are averaged into fb_sum_l/fb_sum_r and fed back with a gain up to 0.85 (magneto_reverb.cpp:137-144). The compounding table in P0-1 applies directly. This matches the measured impulse metrics for Magneto: density 0.552 at 100–200 ms and 0.815 at 200–500 ms, the lowest of any non-ER mode.

There is a second problem in the same mode. Head positions are recomputed every control block with no transition: 

head_delays_l_[i] = period * (float)(i + 1) / (float)n_heads_;

period is derived from params.decay. Sweeping Decay therefore steps every head tap discontinuously every 2 ms. The delay modes solved exactly this with DelayTapTransition (a queued, click-free dual-head crossfade). Magneto does not use it.

Fix. Two changes. Use the allpass interpolator from P0-1 (or ReadAtHighQuality) for the head taps, and route head positions through DelayTapTransition the same way DigitalDelay and LofiDelay do.

Cost: negligible for the interpolator; the transition doubles the tap reads only while a sweep is active

P0-4Poly Octave low-passes its own output at 2.2 kHz, at every Tracking setting

src/daisyfx/hosted/modes/poly_octave_mode.cpp:100-101, 127

The Tracking control ends up as a one-pole low-pass applied to the finished audio: 

const float tracking = (params.speed - 0.05f) / 9.95f;
tracking_coefficient_ = 0.0005f + std::clamp(tracking, 0.0f, 1.0f) * 0.25f;
// ...
tracking_ += tracking_coefficient_ * (out - tracking_);

With params.speed in [0.05, 10] the coefficient spans 0.0005 to 0.2505. Converting to cutoff at 48 kHz:

Tracking settingCoefficientEffective cutoff
Minimum (speed 0.05)0.0005~3.8 Hz
Centre (speed 5)0.125~1.0 kHz
Maximum (speed 10)0.2505~2.2 kHz

At the minimum setting the mode outputs near-silence. At the maximum setting the octave voices are still rolled off from 2.2 kHz upward. There is no position of this control at which Poly Octave passes its own output intact. The comment describes the intent as softening note attacks, which needs a smoother on the gain, not a filter on the signal.

Fix. Apply the smoother to the voice levels, not the audio. Smooth up1_level_, down1_level_ and down2_level_ toward their targets with this coefficient, and leave out untouched.

Cost: zero (three coefficient updates per sample instead of one filter)

P1 — The studio-quality gap

P1-1One hardcoded allpass delay table is shared by every reverb and both channels

src/daisyfx/hosted/dsp/diffuser.h:12

static constexpr size_t kDelays[STAGES] = {73, 113, 193, 313};

Two consequences, both audible.

Shared coloration. Room, Hall, Shimmer, Bloom, Cloud, Nonlinear and Magneto all instantiate Diffuser with this identical table. A Schroeder allpass chain is not spectrally neutral in practice — it imposes a characteristic resonant signature at the reciprocals of its delays. Seven modes that are supposed to sound like different spaces share one fingerprint.

Zero decorrelation from diffusion. Every mode creates diffuser_l_ and diffuser_r_ and initialises both from the same kDelays. For a mono guitar input the two diffusers are bit-identical, so the entire diffusion stage contributes nothing to stereo width. All width comes from the FDN output matrix and the ER tap tables downstream.

Cloud compounds this: it runs two Diffuser instances in series (cloud_reverb.cpp:97-100), both with the same delays. Cascading identical allpass delays doubles the coloration rather than increasing density — the resonances land on top of each other.

Fix. Make the delay table a constructor or Init() parameter instead of a class constant. Give each mode its own mutually co-prime set, and offset the right channel from the left (a common choice is scaling the left set by roughly 1.06–1.13 and rounding to the nearest prime). Cloud's second stage needs a set co-prime with its first. This is a pure retuning exercise: identical structure, identical CPU.

While you are in there: Diffuser never modulates its stages, even though DelayAllpassFilter::ProcessMod() already exists and Plate uses it. A slow, shallow modulation (±2–4 samples at under 1 Hz) on stages 2 and 3 is the standard cure for static allpass ringing.

Cost: zero for retuning; estimated +0.1 % for modulating two stages per channel

P1-2No modulation mode uses the band-limited interpolator

chorus, flanger and rotary — all call ReadAt() (4-point Hermite)

ModeLocationTap behaviour
Choruschorus_mode.cpp:114-116, 135-136, 147-148Continuously modulated, 3 taps
Flangerflanger_mode.cpp:135-136Continuously modulated, in a feedback loop at regen up to 0.95
Rotaryrotary_mode.cpp:132-137Continuously modulated, 4 Doppler taps

These run at 48 kHz, so the loss is milder than in the tank, but from the P0-1 table Hermite still costs −0.53 dB at 10 kHz and −2.53 dB at 15 kHz per read. Flanger is the worst case: the whole point of a flanger is the high-frequency jet, and that tap is inside a 0.95 feedback loop, so the loss compounds.

Fix. Switch these taps to ReadAtHighQuality. The delay modes already follow exactly this policy — ReadNearest when the tap is static, ReadAtHighQuality when it moves. Apply the same rule here.

Cost: estimated +0.3 to 0.5 % of one core for flanger (2 taps) and chorus (3 taps) combined

P1-3The Shimmer pitch shifter has no anti-alias filter, inside a feedback loop

src/daisyfx/hosted/dsp/pitch_shifter.cpp:72-74, driven from shimmer_reverb.cpp:110-111

PitchShifter::Process() reads its grain buffer at ratio_ samples per output sample. For an upward shift the read is a decimation, and there is no low-pass before it. At a +12 semitone shift (ratio_ = 2.0) everything above fs/4 folds. The shifter runs at the 24 kHz tank rate, so that boundary is 6 kHz.

Shimmer feeds the shifted signal back into the FDN (pitch_fb_l_, shimmer_reverb.cpp:103-104), so the aliased components are shifted again on the next circulation, and again after that. Aliasing products are inharmonic, so this accumulates as a metallic, detuned haze rather than as musical shimmer.

Fix. Low-pass the shifter input at fs/(2·ratio) whenever ratio_ > 1. One Svf in LP mode per shifter is enough; set its cutoff in SetShift(), which already runs at control rate.

Related, in the same mode: params.mod controls both the shimmer send level and the FDN modulation depth (shimmer_reverb.cpp:78, 101). Those are unrelated parameters sharing one knob. Consider moving the FDN depth to a fixed sensible value.

Cost: estimated +0.1 % (two one-pole or SVF filters at 24 kHz)

P1-4Phaser and Vibe sweep linearly in allpass coefficient, not in frequency

phaser_mode.cpp:51, 99 and vibe_mode.cpp:17-21, 80

Both modes modulate the allpass coefficient a directly with the LFO. The mapping from a to notch frequency is strongly nonlinear:

Coefficient aNotch frequency
−0.99~76 Hz
−0.95~391 Hz
−0.50~5.1 kHz
−0.01~11.8 kHz

A symmetric LFO swing in a therefore produces a wildly asymmetric sweep in pitch: fast through the top of its range, crawling through the bottom. Analogue phasers use OTA or JFET elements whose control response is close to exponential in frequency, which is why they sweep evenly to the ear. This is the most likely reason both modes sound lopsided compared to a reference unit.

Fix. Sweep in log frequency and convert. Compute f = f_lo · pow(f_hi/f_lo, lfo01), then g = tan(πf/fs), then a = (1−g)/(1+g). Do not call tanf per sample — use the same precomputed, log-spaced lookup table pattern that FilterDelay already implements in filter_delay.cpp:14-26. That table is 257 entries with linear interpolation between them and costs two loads and a multiply-add.

Cost: roughly neutral; the table lookup replaces the arithmetic already being done

P1-5Nothing is oversampled around any saturation stage

dsp/waveshaper.h, dsp/bbd_emulator.h:71, and every mode that drives them

WaveShaper applies drive up to 16× through a tanh-style curve with no oversampling. Every harmonic it generates above Nyquist folds back as an inharmonic tone. The affected modes:

ModeStageRateNotes
Tape Delaysat_ in the feedback loop48 kHzCompounds per repeat
Bucket Brigade DelayBbdEmulator::Process48 kHzDrive up to 8×, in the loop
Lo-fi Delaybit crush + decimate48 kHzIntentional; only 1-pole AA before decimation
Spring Reverbsat_, drive up to 8×24 kHzFolds from 12 kHz — worst case
Rotarydrive_, Tube curve48 kHzAsymmetric, so even harmonics too
Auto Swellhard clip at ±1.048 kHzSee below
Tape Delayhard clip at ±2.0 on write48 kHztape_delay.cpp:125-126

Auto Swell is the sharpest case. It multiplies by curved * (1 + params.depth), up to 2×, then hard-clips: 

if (wet_l >  1.0f) wet_l =  1.0f;
if (wet_l < -1.0f) wet_l = -1.0f;

A hard clip is an infinite-order nonlinearity. On normal playing levels this will engage, and it produces full-bandwidth aliasing. Every other saturation point in the codebase uses a smooth curve; this one and the Tape write clamp are the exceptions.

Fix, in order of value.

  • Replace both hard clips with soft_clip_tanh. It already exists in fast_math.h, and its derivative reaches exactly zero at |x| = 3, so it is C¹ continuous at the clamp — no discontinuity to alias from. Free, and removes the worst offender.
  • Oversample Spring's saturator 2×. It runs at 24 kHz where folding starts at 12 kHz, and HalfbandDecimator2x/HalfbandInterpolator2x already exist in dsp/halfband_resampler.h. Spring measures 45.4 µs (3.4 % of budget), so there is room.
  • Leave Lo-fi and Destroyer alone. Their aliasing is the intended effect.

Cost — soft clip swap: zero Cost — Spring 2×: estimated +1.5 to 2 %, taking Spring to roughly 5 % of budget

P1-6Reverb tap tables are recomputed per control block with no crossfade

room_reverb.cpp:108-117, reflections_reverb.cpp:95-118, magneto_reverb.cpp:75-94

The delay bank learned this lesson. DelayTapTransition gives every delay mode a queued, click-free 50 ms dual-head crossfade whenever a tap moves. The reverbs got none of it.

  • Room rounds every ER tap to an integer sample scaled by Size: lround(kErTaps[i].delay_samples * size_scale). Sweeping Size steps eight taps at once, every 2 ms.
  • Reflections rebuilds all 16 taps every control block, with delays derived from param2 and gains multiplied by params.decay. Both Loc X and Decay sweeps produce stepped tap positions and stepped gains.
  • Magneto — see P0-3.

Fix. Gains are the easy half: slew gain_l_/gain_r_ inside EarlyReflections toward their targets over roughly 20 ms instead of assigning them in SetTaps(). Positions are the harder half; the cheapest correct answer is to reuse DelayTapTransition per tap group rather than per tap.

Cost: estimated +0.2 % for gain slewing; position crossfade doubles reads only during a sweep

P1-7A DC blocker sits inside every FDN feedback loop and shortens low-frequency decay

fdn.cpp:192, 253 with dsp/dc_blocker.h:13

Fdn::Process() runs dc_[i].Process(v[i]) on every line, inside the loop. The blocker is a one-pole high-pass at 5.35 Hz. One pass is nearly transparent, but the signal makes one pass per circulation.

FrequencyLoss per passAfter ~66 passes (2 s tail, 30 ms loop)
50 Hz−0.049 dB−3.2 dB
100 Hz−0.012 dB−0.8 dB

The result is that bass decays measurably faster than mids, which is the opposite of what a real hall does and works against the SetDampFromRt60Ratio() control that is supposed to own the frequency-dependent decay.

Fix. Move DC blocking out of the loop — one blocker on the FDN output per channel instead of one per line inside the feedback path. That is also cheaper: 2 blockers instead of 8. If you want belt-and-braces protection inside the loop, drop the in-loop cutoff to ~1 Hz, where 66 passes costs under 0.2 dB at 50 Hz.

Cost: negative (8 in-loop blockers become 2 output blockers)

P1-8Plate omits the Dattorro input bandwidth filter, and side signal bypasses diffusion

plate_reverb.cpp:145-153, 178, 194

Plate is the strongest algorithm in the bank — native 48 kHz, faithful topology, correct read-before-write ordering, and the flattest measured impulse response (energy −0.25 dB, L/R correlation −0.078, density 1.000). Two gaps remain against the cited reference.

No input bandwidth filter. Dattorro's design places a one-pole low-pass on the input before the diffusers, controlling how much high frequency enters the tank at all. This implementation feeds the diffusers full-band. The damping filters lp_a_/lp_b_ are inside the tank, so they shape the tail but not the initial diffusion, which reads brighter and harder than a plate should.

Undiffused side injection. side is computed from the raw pre-delay outputs and injected directly into both tanks: 

const float side = 0.5f * (pre_l - pre_r);
// ... later ...
const float ta_in = (hold_ ? 0.0f : s + side) + decay_ * d8_fb;

s has been through four input allpasses; side has been through none. Any stereo content in the source enters the tank as an undiffused transient.

Fix. Add a one-pole low-pass on mid and side before the diffusers, mapped from Tone or from a fixed 9–12 kHz. Route side through its own short diffuser (two allpasses is enough) rather than injecting it raw.

Cost: estimated +0.2 % (two one-poles plus two allpasses at 48 kHz)

P1-9Chorus clears its delay buffer when the sub-mode changes

chorus_mode.cpp:49-52

int new_sub_mode = static_cast<int>(params.p2 * 4.999f);
if (new_sub_mode != sub_mode_) {
    sub_mode_ = new_sub_mode;
    chorus_line_.Reset();   // memset of 2400 floats
}

DelayLineSdram::Reset() zeroes the whole buffer. Sweeping the sub-mode knob through its five positions therefore produces four audible dropouts, and does a 9.6 kB memset on the audio thread each time. Other modes that switch discrete variants (FilterDelay, PatternDelay, SpringReverb, ChoraleReverb) all use hysteresis dead bands and none of them clear state.

Fix. Add a dead band as those modes do, and drop the Reset(). The buffer contents are valid audio history regardless of which sub-mode reads them; the only thing that changes is the tap positions and the pre-colouring.

Cost: negative (removes a 9.6 kB memset from the audio thread)

P1-10Chorus delay base and depth jump at block boundaries

chorus_mode.cpp:62-74 used at 109, 129-130, 141-142

The code comment says the LFO is advanced per sample specifically to avoid block-boundary jumps. It is — but base_samps_ and mod_depth_ are still assigned in Prepare() and used directly per sample. A change in params.p1 moves the base delay by up to 912 samples instantaneously.

On a knob that is fine because the pot is smoothed upstream (POT_SMOOTH). On MIDI CC or expression-pedal automation, which arrives in 128 discrete steps, each step is a 7-sample jump in the delay tap — an audible tick. The delay bank handles precisely this with DelayTapTransition.

Fix. Slew base_samps_ and mod_depth_ per sample toward their targets. A one-pole with a 20–50 ms time constant is enough and costs two multiply-adds.

Cost: estimated under 0.05 %

P1-11The Barber Pole phaser does not rise continuously, and is mono

phaser_mode.cpp:59-93

A barber-pole phaser needs notches that rise (or fall) forever, which requires either true frequency shifting or a set of N staggered chains crossfaded by a monotonically increasing ramp. This implementation uses two chains at 90° and crossfades them with: 

const float t = (lfo_val + 1.0f) * 0.5f;
const float gain_a = fast_sin(t * 1.57079633f);
const float gain_b = fast_sin((1.0f - t) * 1.57079633f);

t is derived from a sine LFO, so it rises and then falls. The crossfade reverses direction every half cycle, which is a normal phaser with a wobble, not a barber pole. The mode also returns {out, out}, discarding the stereo the rest of the phaser produces.

Fix. Drive t from a sawtooth phase accumulator (or use LfoWave::RampUp, which already exists) rather than from the sine, and use three or four chains at evenly staggered phases so the crossfade never has an audible seam. The project already contains dsp/hilbert_transform.h and dsp/band_shifter.h for QuadratureMode, which is the other route to a true barber pole.

Cost: zero for the ramp fix; estimated +0.3 % for two extra 4-stage chains

P2 — Cost, consistency and polish

Wasted CPU

P2-1The halfband resampler multiplies by zero roughly half the time. dsp/halfband_resampler.h:42-49, 78-85
kCoeffs is a true half-band kernel: 15 of its 31 taps are exactly 0.0f. Both convolve() loops run all 31 taps anyway, and each tap computes its index with an integer modulo (write_ + kTaps - 1 - i) % kTaps. HalfbandInterpolator2x::Process() compounds this by pushing a zero and convolving twice, so it does 62 taps of work per output pair. Every half-rate reverb pays this on every sample.
Fix. Skip the zero taps (a polyphase form leaves 8 non-zero taps plus the centre), and round the history buffer to 32 entries so the index is a mask instead of a modulo. Cost: negative — estimated 2× to 3× faster resampling

P2-2FilterMode calls tanf() once per sample. filter_mode.cpp:81
svf_.SetFreq(cutoff) in the per-sample LFO path calls tanf() internally (svf.h:22). FilterDelay solved exactly this with a 257-entry log-spaced g table and an explicit comment saying so (filter_delay.cpp:14-26, 95). Fix. Reuse that table. Cost: negative, estimated −0.3 %

P2-3Phaser calls libm tanhf() two or three times per sample. phaser_mode.cpp:71-72, 106, 126
soft_clip_tanh() in fast_math.h is the intended replacement and is already used by Flanger and FilterDelay. Cost: negative

P2-4Nonlinear reverb calls std::exp and std::cos per sample. nonlinear_reverb.cpp:10-32, called at line 142
shape_gain() runs libm transcendentals on every tank sample. shape_phase_ advances at a fixed rate, so all six shapes are one-dimensional functions of a bounded phase. Fix. Precompute a 256-entry table per shape at Init() and interpolate. Cost: negative

P2-5The Flanger wow-and-flutter drift is roughly 0.006 samples — inaudible. flanger_mode.cpp:117-120, 127-128
drift_l_ += 0.0002f * (lcg_to_float(rand_state_) - drift_l_) is a one-pole on uniform noise. Its steady-state standard deviation is σin·√(a/2) = 0.577 × 0.01 ≈ 0.0058 samples, or about 0.12 µs of delay — roughly two orders of magnitude below audibility. It costs two LCG steps and four multiply-adds per sample and delivers nothing.
Fix. Either scale it up by ~100× (to a few samples of wow, which is what tape actually does) or delete it. Cost: negative either way

Consistency and correctness

P2-6The BBD soft clip is missing the bound its two siblings have. dsp/bbd_emulator.h:71
The same Padé approximant appears three times in the codebase. fast_math.h:48 and waveshaper.h:31 both guard it with if (x >= 3.0f) return 1.0f. BbdEmulator does not:
const float sat = x * (27.0f + x * x) / (27.0f + 9.0f * x * x);
Beyond |x| = 3 this approximant is not a limiter — it grows without bound, asymptotically as x/9. Loud transients pass through at 1/9 gain with the wrong harmonic structure instead of being limited.
Fix. Call soft_clip_tanh() and delete the third copy. Cost: zero

P2-7Independent LFO jitter destroys the fixed phase relationships it is applied to. chorus_mode.cpp:15-16, rotary_mode.cpp:36-47, flanger_mode.cpp:88-89
Each of these creates two or three Lfo objects, sets a fixed phase offset between them (120° for Chorus, 90° for Rotary's microphone pair and Flanger's stereo spread), then calls SetJitter() on each one separately. Lfo::Process() perturbs phase_inc_ from its own PRNG at every wrap (lfo.cpp:78-84), so the offsets random-walk apart and never return. Rotary's comment describes "two microphones placed 90° apart"; after a few seconds they are not.
Fix. Derive the offset LFOs from one phase accumulator plus a constant offset, and apply jitter once to that shared accumulator. Cost: negative

P2-8Three delay modes lack the feedback DC blocker the other seven have. Duck, Swell and Trem delays
Digital, Tape, Bucket Brigade, Lo-fi, Dual, Filter and Pattern all run dc_fb_l_/dc_fb_r_ on the feedback signal. DuckDelay (duck_delay.cpp:87-88), SwellDelay (swell_delay.cpp:122-123) and TremDelay (trem_delay.cpp:70-71) do not. The ToneFilter in each of those loops has non-zero DC gain, so offset can accumulate across repeats and eat headroom.
Fix. Add the two blockers to match. Cost: negligible

P2-9Fdn::Process is duplicated and the two copies have already diverged. fdn.cpp:185-244 and 246-306
The mono and stereo overloads are ~60 near-identical lines each. They have already drifted: the mono version folds the hold mute into in_scaled, the stereo version does it inline in the loop. Any fix from this document that touches the FDN has to be applied twice, and will eventually be applied once.
Fix. Make the mono overload call the stereo one with {input, input}, or factor the shared body into a private helper taking the per-line injection. Cost: zero

P2-10Filter and Formant modes update resonant coefficients only at block rate. filter_mode.cpp:55-59, formant_mode.cpp:27-53
FilterMode's envelope sub-modes (Env+, Env−) compute the cutoff per sample but only apply it once per block via Prepare(). With Q up to 20 that is a stepped auto-wah — the mode most sensitive to zipper noise is the one that has it. FormantMode recomputes both formant banks' biquad coefficients every block at Q up to 10, and steps blend_a_/blend_b_ with them.
Fix. For FilterMode, use the g-table from P2-2 and set it per sample as the LFO path already does. For FormantMode, slew the blend per sample. Cost: estimated +0.1 %

P2-11FilterMode switches filter topology on a hard threshold with no dead band. filter_mode.cpp:47-49
params.tone selects LP below 0.4, BP between 0.4 and 0.6, HP above 0.6 — and also sets base_hz_ on line 41. Crossing 0.4 or 0.6 swaps the output tap instantaneously, which clicks. FilterDelay handles the same choice with explicit 0.03 dead bands and a comment explaining why (filter_delay.cpp:67-81).
Fix. Copy the dead-band pattern. Cost: zero

P2-12Quadrature's FM mode is a frequency shifter labelled as vibrato. quadrature_mode.cpp:76-81, 101-107
The code integrates inst_freq = params.depth * 80.0f * lfo_val into the carrier phase and performs single-sideband rotation. That is an additive frequency shift oscillating around 0 Hz. Pitch vibrato is a multiplicative ratio. On a single note the difference is subtle; on a chord the additive shift destroys the harmonic relationships between the notes. The comment claims "pitch wobble via single-sideband rotation", which is not achievable this way.
Fix. Either rename the sub-mode to something honest ("Warble" or "Shift LFO"), or implement vibrato with a modulated delay line as Chorus already does. Cost: zero to rename

Musical and mixing behaviour

P2-13Both tremolos lose 6 dB at full depth with no make-up gain. vintage_trem_mode.cpp:71, pattern_trem_mode.cpp:56
gain = 1.0f - depth_ * contour with contour in [0, 1] gives a mean gain of 0.5 at full depth. Engaging the tremolo therefore drops the level by 6 dB. Studio and pedal tremolos almost always compensate, either with a fixed make-up or a depth-tracked one.
Fix. Multiply the output by 1 / (1 - depth * 0.5), or expose a level control. Cost: one multiply

P2-14Several modes cancel or comb when summed to mono. TremDelay, and the fixed stereo offsets across the delay bank
TremDelay modulates L and R in anti-phase (trem_delay.cpp:54-55). At mod_dep = 1 the tremolo vanishes entirely in a mono sum. Separately, Digital, Tape, Dual, Filter and Swell delays all offset the right head by a fixed 150, 61, 47 or 31 samples, which combs at 160 Hz, 787 Hz, 1.02 kHz and 1.55 kHz respectively when folded to mono.
Fix. Not necessarily a bug — it is a real design trade-off. Worth a documented decision and, ideally, a mono-compatibility check in the test suite. Cost: n/a

P2-15Tape Delay's Grit control divides feedback by up to 15. tape_delay.cpp:116
return tapeLp / (1.0f + params.grit * params.grit * 14.0f);
Turning Grit up therefore kills the repeats. Grit and Repeats fight each other, which makes both hard to dial. This reads as a runaway-prevention hack, but FeedbackLimiter and the ±2.0 write clamp are already downstream doing that job.
Fix. Normalise the saturator for unity RMS through the loop instead of dividing by a constant, so Grit changes harmonic content without changing loop gain. BbdEmulator already does exactly this (bbd_emulator.h:74: return sat / drive;). Cost: zero

P2-16Early reflections have no per-tap high-frequency absorption. dsp/early_reflections.h:35-45, tables in room_reverb.cpp, hall_reverb.cpp, reflections_reverb.cpp
Every ER tap applies a flat, frequency-independent gain. Real reflections lose high frequency to both air absorption and surface absorption, and the later the reflection the more it loses. Flat taps are the main reason a tap-based early field reads as "metallic" rather than as a room.
Fix. Give each tap a one-pole low-pass whose cutoff falls with tap delay. With 8 taps (Room) or 16 (Reflections) that is 8 or 16 one-poles per channel. Cost: estimated +0.3 % for Room, +0.6 % for Reflections

P2-17Seven modes discard stereo entirely. Vibe, Filter, Formant, Destroyer, Poly Octave, Quadrature (modes 1–3), Phaser (Barber Pole)
All return {x, x}. For Vibe this is authentic — a Uni-Vibe is a mono circuit. For Filter, Formant, Destroyer and Poly Octave it is a missed opportunity: each already has the structure for a second channel, and Filter in particular would benefit from the quadrature-LFO treatment that Phaser's normal path already uses.
Fix. Lowest effort with the largest effect: give FilterMode a second SVF driven by a 90°-offset LFO, matching what phaser_mode.cpp:120-138 already does. Cost: estimated +0.2 % per mode converted

Decisions that changed during implementation

  • P0-1 uses the band-limited reader, not the allpass interpolator this document originally recommended. A first-order allpass is magnitude-perfect and cheaper, but its coefficient jumps every time the modulated tap crosses an integer sample boundary — roughly 80 times a second at the FDN's depth and rate. That is fine for a fixed fractional delay and wrong for a moving one. ReadAtHighQuality has no state, no discontinuity, and was already proven by all ten delay modes.
  • P1-7 keeps a DC blocker inside the FDN loop, at 1 Hz instead of 5.35 Hz, rather than moving it to the output. During Hold the feedback gain is exactly 1.0, so an in-loop blocker is what stops DC accumulating without bound. At 1 Hz a 2 s tail loses under 0.1 dB at 50 Hz instead of 3.2 dB.
  • P2-13 was over-scoped in the original finding. Make-up gain was applied to the continuous tremolo only. A gated pattern already sits at unity while its gate is open, so there is no level drop to correct and a boost would only flatten the pattern's dynamics. The continuous tremolo compensates RMS rather than mean — matching the mean would put peaks 6 dB over unity at full depth — and its output is soft-limited.
  • P2-17 converted FilterMode to true stereo and left the rest mono. Vibe is authentically a mono circuit. Formant, Destroyer, Poly Octave and Quadrature remain mono by choice, not oversight.

Level changes worth knowing about

Three fixes cost broadband level by construction, because they add filtering that was not there before. Plate loses 1.5 dB to its new input bandwidth filter (P1-8); Room and Hall lose roughly 1 dB each to per-tap early-reflection absorption (P2-16). These are the intended cost of the change, not regressions, and the engine's per-mode reverbOutputGain table is the right place to recalibrate if you want the old levels back.

Follow-up: Poly Octave level

Reported after the first pass: Poly Octave sounded considerably quieter. Measured through the full engine at catalog defaults, the change in this document made it slightly louder, not quieter — 0.20462 RMS against 0.19946 before, about +0.2 dB. Driven harder (Octave Up at full, Tracking above centre) it is up to 11 dB louder, because the old code was low-passing its own output.

Chasing it did find a real defect, and it was not in this document. Poly Octave's 6× resamplers are cycfi ring_buffers backed by std::array. The std::array overload of init_store() sets the index mask but never zeroes the storage — unlike the std::vector overload beside it, which does: 

template <typename T, std::size_t N>
void init_store(std::array<T, N>& _data, std::size_t& _mask)
{
   _mask = _data.size() - 1;     // _data is never cleared
}

Nothing called ring_buffer::clear(), so the mode ran its first samples of uninitialised memory through the resampler FIR taps and then into the IIR output shelves, which rang on afterwards. Measured peaks reached 4.6×1033, and the magnitude varied with whatever happened to be in memory. At the lowest Tracking setting the mode ended up emitting exactly zero, permanently.

Addition: Whammy mode, and the pitch shifter rebuild it forced

A new ModModeId::Whammy implements both DigiTech families — ten Whammy presets (unison at heel, the named interval at toe) and nine Harmony presets (dry plus one voice, the pedal morphing between two fixed chromatic intervals). P1 is the pedal position and is the control to assign to an expression input.

It does not use the Poly Octave engine. BandShifter produces its voices with double- and half-angle identities, so it is structurally limited to ×2, ÷2 and ÷4; a fifth is ratio 1.4983 and simply is not expressible. The granular shifter already in the tree takes arbitrary, continuously varying ratios — but it turned out to need real work first.

The shifter was badly out of tune, and nobody had noticed

Each grain restart jumps the read pointer back by (ratio−1) × grain samples. When that distance is not a whole number of input periods, the leftover phase biases the output pitch by leftover × grain rate. The consequence is a detune that depends on which note you play. Predicted and measured (the model reproduced 4 of 7 spot measurements exactly), at +12 semitones:

Detune, before+115+56−37−38+38−4

cents. Across all intervals the range was −923 to +829 cents.

This is masked in the two modes that use the shifter today: Shimmer only asks for +12 and +7 and buries the result in a reverb wash, and Chorus detune moves it by 0.3 of a semitone. It is fatal for anything that has to play an interval.

Fixed with waveform-similarity overlap-add

Rather than jumping a fixed distance, the restart position is chosen by searching back up to one low-E period for the point whose run-up best matches the grain it is about to overlap. That removes the phase discontinuity and with it the detune.

Two details mattered more than the search itself:

  • Match against the overlap partner, not the grain's own history. Self-matching left the two grains free to land in anti-phase, which cancelled the carrier and left the output as a pair of sidebands at ± the grain rate. At 330 Hz the target pitch measured 0.025 while the sidebands measured 0.24. Matching the partner restored the carrier to 0.436 and dropped the sidebands to 0.005 — 34 dB.
  • Coarse pass then refine. Scanning every candidate put roughly 45k multiply-accumulates into the one sample where a grain restarts, which showed as Shimmer's p99 going from 8.3 to 77.8 µs. A stride-8 first pass with a local refine cannot step over a correlation peak at any pitch this is used at, and brought p99 back to 15.7 µs.

One idea that measured worse and was reverted

Four overlapping grains instead of two, to halve the grain-rate roughness. It cost 9 to 34 dB through cancellation: naive overlap-add sums mutually incoherent segments, so more grains means more cancellation. That technique belongs to phase-locked resynthesis, not to this structure. Grain count is fixed at two and the knob was removed; grain size stayed configurable, which is the control that actually helps.

What was checked and found correct

Recording these so they are not re-litigated:

  • Control-rate timing is correct. Fdn::PrepareBlock() and Lfo::PrepareBlock() both assume 48 elapsed samples. controlInterval() returns 96 host frames for half-rate reverbs and 48 otherwise (DaisyFxProcessor.cpp:217-218), so both see exactly 48 of their own samples. The assumption holds, and it is independent of the audio callback size.
  • Denormals are handled. ScopedDenormalGuard sets FPCR.FZ on AArch64 and MXCSR FTZ/DAZ on x86, and restores the previous state. No per-filter denormal guards are needed.
  • soft_clip_tanh is C¹ continuous at its clamp. The Padé approximant x(27+x²)/(27+9x²) has derivative exactly zero at x = 3, so the transition into the hard clamp introduces no slope discontinuity. Well chosen.
  • Hadamard scaling is orthonormal. 4-line uses 0.5, 8-line composes two 4-line stages with 1/√2, giving 1/√8 overall. Energy-preserving.
  • The Plate read/write ordering is correct. All output and feedback taps are read before any tank write in the same sample, and d5_.ReadAt(kD5Delay) after d5_.Write() reads past the freshly written sample as the comment claims.
  • The pitch shifter never laps its write head. The restart compensation at pitch_shifter.cpp:79-83 keeps the read pointer 4 samples behind the writer at grain end for ratios above 1, and behind by construction for ratios below 1.
  • Spring's param2 thresholds are right. The 1.4/1.6/2.4/2.6 dead bands look wrong against a normalised control, but PARAM2_SPRING is {1.0f, 3.0f, 0.0f}, so the mode does receive a 1–3 physical range and all three springs are reachable.
  • Hall's mid-EQ band is where the comment says. The two one-poles at 0.48 and 0.063 form a 249 Hz – 2.5 kHz band at the 24 kHz tank rate.
  • The delay bank's static/moving tap policy is correct and consistently applied across all ten modes. This is the pattern the other two families should adopt.
