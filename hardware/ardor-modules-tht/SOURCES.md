## ADS1115 breakout pin translation

The carrier pad numbers follow the **ADC's logical pin functions**, not the header's left-to-right ordinal. Viewing the ADS1115-M component side from above, with its single header along the lower edge and VDD at the left:

| Row, left → right | Carrier footprint pad numbers |
|---|---|
| VDD, GND, SCL, SDA, ADDR, ALRT, A0, A1, A2, A3 | 8 (3.3 V), 3 (GND), 10 (clock), 9 (data), 1 (address), 2 (NC), 4 (wiper ADC), 5 (reference ADC), 6 (GND), 7 (GND) |

The row is 22.86 mm between its first and last pin; the module extends 2.3 mm left of VDD, 1.8 mm below the row and 15.2 mm above it. The footprint reserves the full 27.5 × 17 mm body plus a 0.5 mm courtyard margin; no THT component is placed under it. The two module mounting holes are documented on F.Fab, not drilled into the carrier. Use one straight ten-pin header/socket; check total stacked height and support the assembly during insertion/removal.

Onboard 10 kΩ SDA/SCL pull-ups remain fitted; JP303/304 control only the extra carrier 2.2 kΩ pair. Leave those shunts off initially. The onboard ADDR-to-ground 10 kΩ resistor also remains: JP302 directly selects GND (0x48) or 3.3 V (0x49), drawing approximately 0.33 mA when HIGH. ALRT is unused; A2/A3 remain tied to ground. Only the former Adafruit-specific AVDD/AGND carrier terminals are removed.

The supplier schematic labels its ADC ADS1015 while the product title and chip MPN caption specify ADS1115. Commission the actual module as a 16-bit ADS1115: inspect identity and verify known inputs and single-shot busy time. At DR bits `100`, [TI ADS1115](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1115.pdf) specifies 128 SPS (about 7.8 ms conversion), while [TI ADS1015](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1015.pdf) specifies 1600 SPS (about 0.625 ms). Allow oscillator/startup and bus timing tolerances. I²C address detection alone does not resolve the device-identity discrepancy.

## Electrical calculations, not measured performance

For the headphone circuit, `gain = -10k / 20k = -0.5` unloaded. With 32 Ω headphones, `32 || 1000 = 31.008 Ω`, and the 10 Ω isolation resistor gives `-0.5 × 31.008 / (10 + 31.008) = -0.3781` nominal midband gain. For 1 Vrms input this is approximately 4.47 mW/channel into 32 Ω, with roughly 17.25 mA peak total output current including the bleeder. Both channels invert together. Input coupling is 10 µF bipolar / 20 kΩ: a nominal 0.796 Hz pole. The feedback 10 kΩ / 100 pF pole is about 159 kHz. Nominal output coupling has an 8.26 Hz pole at 32 Ω including the series resistor and bleeder. These are ideal small-signal calculations, not measured audio performance.

The OPA1656 common-mode input range is from V− to V+−2.25 V. At a 5 V rail the 2.5 V input bias is inside this range, with 0.25 V nominal upper margin. At the minimum 4.75 V supply, allowing ±1% divider resistors and the bead's maximum 20 mΩ DC resistance at the full 150 mA budget, the upper margin remains above 0.09 V. The inverting topology keeps both inputs at VREF rather than making them follow the audio signal. Output swing and distortion are not guaranteed for 32 Ω by the datasheet's 2 kΩ or 600 Ω specifications. The typical ±100 mA figure is **short-circuit current**, not a clean continuous-output rating; verify the actual output at both supply limits.

At startup the reference divider has nominal `(10k || 10k) × 47µF = 0.235 s` settling time, and the muted 1 kΩ bleeder charges its output capacitor through the 10 Ω isolation resistor with `(1000 + 10) × 470µF = 0.4747 s`. Five seconds provides more than eight output time constants at +20% capacitance and +1% resistance. The input coupling/reference dynamics can affect the startup waveform, so this simple timing calculation does not certify click-free operation. The host supplies the delay and must mute before power-down; there is no timer on the PCB. Measure DC and switching transients into dummy loads before connecting headphones.

Local feedback resistors use upright axial footprints at 2.54 mm pitch to keep their summing-node connections short. C641 uses a small 2.5 mm-pitch THT ceramic body and a fixed short front-layer connection to supply pin 8. Fixed local feedback routes are part of the placement source, ahead of general routing. Follow TI's decoupling/layout guidance and bench-check stability with real headphone cables, representative capacitive loads, unplugged outputs and TRS insertion shorts. Retain the 10 Ω isolation resistors outside the feedback loop.
