Ardor flexible-routing feasibility probe
========================================

Date: 2026-09-08
Target: Raspberry Pi 4B, AArch64, 4 cores, 927 MiB RAM
Kernel: 6.18.37-v8 PREEMPT
Clock: 1.5 GHz fixed during the runs; all CPU governors were performance
Audio/callback CPU: 2, SCHED_FIFO/60 in the probe (production callback is FIFO/70)
Worker CPUs: 0, 1, and 3, SCHED_FIFO/69, all affinity setup calls succeeded
Sample rate: 48 kHz
Cabinet probe: deterministic dense 8192-sample mono IR
Models: the five NAM files installed under /opt/ardor-pedal/models on the Pi

The probe is tests/routing_feasibility.cpp and remains a benchmark rather than
the audio callback. It uses the real RuntimeChain, the prepared
FlexibleRoutingProgram owner, and the existing DualRigProcessor. It measures
serial chains, the current two-lane worker implementation, and callback-
participating N-lane worker shapes. All timed buffers and worker threads are
created before timing starts.

All isolated runs stopped S99ardor-pedal with an EXIT trap and restarted it on
every exit. The final device performance probe passed: no callback overruns,
gaps, worker faults, DSP non-finite values, or block-size mismatches. The
production service is running again.

Headline sustained result (JCM800, 2,000 timed blocks, 64 frames)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The 64-frame deadline is 1,333.333 us. The long-run files are the authoritative
stress samples; the shorter 500/1,000-block files are useful for seeing the
best-case distribution but are not admission evidence.

case                                      mean us   p99 us   p999 us  misses
serial 1 NAM                                447.9     464.4     654.6       0
serial 2 NAM                                945.9   1,115.2   1,270.0       0
serial 2 NAM + one cab                    1,122.5   1,208.6   1,260.5       0
parallel 2 NAM, no cab                     961.7   1,242.6   1,413.4       5
parallel 3 NAM, no cab                   1,072.5   1,307.7   1,423.6       6
parallel 2 NAM + cab per lane            1,393.0   2,039.1   2,310.4   1,791
parallel 3 NAM + cab per lane            1,561.7   2,185.6   2,634.5   2,000
parallel 3 NAM + one shared callback cab 1,425.0   2,104.3   2,353.1   1,983

The no-cab worker rows demonstrate that aggregate CPU capacity is not the only
constraint: direct cross-core dispatch/wait has a long-tail wake/processing
cost. The cabinet rows add a second, larger critical-path cost. A short run can
look healthy (for example, the 500-block two-lane NAM+cab p99 was about 725 us),
so any admission rule must use sustained p99.9 data plus margin, not a mean or a
short smoke run.

Latest prepared-owner slice (JCM800, 1,000 paced blocks, 64 frames)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The production-facing FlexibleRoutingProgram now owns the per-lane
RuntimeChains and is installable in PedalEngine through an explicit control-
thread API. The real-NAM row below exercises that owner with two panned lanes,
a centre dry path, and the bounded pipeline:

case                                      mean us   p99 us   p999 us  misses
prepared program, 2 NAM, stereo mix       1,145.7   1,224.0   1,556.5       1
prepared program, 2 NAM + shared cab      1,351.7   2,010.6   2,343.5     534

This is a lifecycle/ownership validation, not a 64-frame admission pass: the
p99.9 tail still exceeds the 1,333.333 us budget. The default program policy
rejects pipelined per-lane convolution and currently rejects pipelined
post-join stages unless the dedicated post-stage worker is explicitly selected.
The shared-cab direct row shows why: putting a dense IR on the callback misses
the 64-frame budget repeatedly.

Dedicated post-stage worker experiment (JCM800, 1,000 paced blocks, 64 frames)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The new `ParallelStereoStageExecutor` runs the ordered shared-cab chain on a
third persistent worker (CPU3), with a bounded two-slot ring and an explicit
bypass before the first completed generation. The lane workers remain direct;
the post stage is the only newly pipelined component:

case                                      mean us   p99 us   p999 us  misses
prepared direct lanes + shared cab       1,347.0   1,996.0   2,458.4     518
prepared direct lanes + cab post-pipeline 1,186.4   1,265.6   1,466.2       3

The post-pipeline row is materially better: only three callback deadline
misses in 1,000 paced blocks and one initial post-stage bypass. Its p99 is
inside the budget, while p99.9 still needs margin and longer-duration evidence.
This validates the architecture as a feasible next admission candidate, not as
permission to expose arbitrary post chains without a measured policy.

The prepared graph now also exposes optional allocation-free diagnostics for
each lane and the post stage: callback calls/total/max nanoseconds, requested
and observed CPU, direct handoff wait totals/maxima, ring submission misses,
and initial underflows. Timing collection is opt-in so normal audio callbacks
do not pay for clock reads.

The targeted 60-second paced run (45,000 timed 64-frame blocks) made the tail
decision clearer, but those first files were collected before the ring-slot
ownership race was found and fixed. They are retained as historical artifacts,
not admission evidence. The corrected admission placement is explicit: lane
worker CPU0 and post-stage worker CPU3. The corrected run is:

case                                      mean us   p99 us   p999 us  misses
corrected graph, service stopped             856.7   1,245.9   1,504.4     126
corrected graph, live service                942.5   1,303.7   1,565.0     285

The corrected long runs still have a p99.9 above the 1,333.333 us budget and
126–285 deadline misses. They are therefore not a zero-fault admission;
the next step is to validate the worker-published handoff below and define a
headroom policy, not UI exposure. The live-service repeat is slightly faster
on average but has the same tail shape, so service contention is not the only
cause.

Worker-published handoff experiment (JCM800, CPU0 lane / CPU3 post worker)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The callback-side ring-to-snapshot copy was removed from the lane workers and
the serial post worker. Each worker now writes a preallocated publication ring;
an acquire/release completion marker transfers ownership, and the callback
acknowledges a publication only after the graph mixer or host-output copy has
finished. A worker waits rather than overwriting an unconsumed publication
slot, so the optimization remains bounded and race-free even when completion
notifications are observed late.

The short service-stopped matrix improved the 64-frame row from mean 526.0 us /
p99.9 771.9 us to 512.2 us / 662.6 us, with zero misses. The 32- and 128-frame
rows were also inside budget:

case / quantum                           mean us   p99 us   p999 us  misses
worker-published, 32 frames                 288.9     307.4     346.2       0
worker-published, 64 frames                 512.2     569.9     662.6       0
worker-published, 128 frames              1,020.0   1,199.9   1,449.5       0

The corrected 60-second stopped-service run improved the mean from 856.7 us to
811.3 us, but its p99.9 was 1,556.3 us with 166 misses. That is better average
throughput but not a production admission: the long-tail deadline still fails
the 1,333.333 us budget. The handoff is therefore a useful implementation
optimization, not evidence that arbitrary routed plans are ready for UI
exposure.

The no-cab 64-frame follow-up also completed without publication submission
misses. The two-lane prepared program pipeline measured 544.5 us mean / 600.7 us
p99 / 1,310.0 us p99.9 with one deadline miss in 2,000 blocks; the three-lane
executor pipeline measured 1,215.1 us mean / 1,283.4 us p99 / 1,542.4 us p99.9
with eight misses. This confirms that the acknowledgment protocol is not
silently stalling the ordinary lane graph, while still showing why three active
NAM lanes need a conservative tail admission rule.

Core-placement and telemetry gate
---------------------------------

The probe now has an opt-in `--telemetry` mode. It reports each lane/post
callback's process mean/max, requested and observed CPU, direct handoff wait,
and ring counters without changing the normal benchmark output. In the short
CPU2/CPU0/CPU3 sample, lane zero averaged 503 us, lane one 505 us, the post
stage 137 us, and direct handoff wait 8 us. The long instrumented sample saw a
lane-zero maximum of 2,202 us and a handoff maximum of 961 us, which explains
why end-to-end p99.9 is scheduler-sensitive even when average worker cost is
stable.

Worker placement matters. With audio CPU2, lane worker CPU0, and post worker
CPU3, four short placement samples were clean when the post worker stayed on
CPU3; moving the post worker to CPU1 produced 10–12 deadline misses and p99.9
around 1,574 us. A 45,000-block repeat with audio CPU1 and workers CPU0/CPU3
was clean twice while audio CPU2 produced 163–167 misses in two comparable
stopped-service runs:

condition / placement                    mean us   p99 us   p999 us  misses
stopped, audio2 / lane0 / post3 (run 1)    596.2   1,238.0   1,652.3     163
stopped, audio2 / lane0 / post3 (run 2)    740.5   1,260.8   1,530.5     167
stopped, audio1 / lane0 / post3 (run 1)    588.2     641.1     897.2       0
stopped, audio1 / lane0 / post3 (run 2)    554.4     612.1     787.9       0
live, audio1 / lane0 / post3              568.1     623.4     719.5       0

The audio-CPU result is strong enough to make CPU1 the preferred candidate for
the next scheduler image/configuration experiment, but not enough to change
the shipped service arguments yet: it needs repeats across all installed NAM
models and thermal conditions. The current service remained on CPU2 during
these tests and reported zero overruns, gaps, worker faults, and non-finite
blocks.

That model/thermal screen has now completed. All five installed models passed
10,000 live-service blocks on audio CPU1 with zero deadline misses; the slowest
short-screen mean was 581.9 us and its p99.9 was 720.8 us. The slowest model
(Ampeg SHO) then passed a full 45,000-block live run at mean 570.4 us, p99.9
698.7 us, maximum 880.0 us, and zero misses. This makes the bounded two-lane
NAM plus shared post-cab topology feasible at 64 frames when its core contract
is explicit: audio CPU1, lane worker CPU0, post worker CPU3. It does not admit
arbitrary lane counts or permit changing the shipped CPU2 service assignment
without a separate deployment/thermal validation.

Lifecycle replacement/reset gate
---------------------------------

The control-thread lifecycle smoke now installs the pipelined two-lane plus
dedicated post-stage graph, submits eight blocks, resets it, submits another
block, and repeats that sequence across eight prepared-program replacements.
The host test passed, and the same ARM64 binary passed on the Pi. This covers
worker teardown before callback-context destruction, generation reset, stale
ring-output invalidation, and engine clear/replacement ownership. It does not
make concurrent preset replacement legal: the caller must still stop the audio
callback before install, reset, or clear.

ThreadSanitizer initially found ring-slot ownership races in this gate: a
worker could begin writing a recycled lane/post slot while the callback still
read or copied that slot's previous output. The worker-published handoff now
uses a second bounded publication ring plus an explicit callback-consumed
generation; worker reuse waits for that generation, and reset/shutdown release
parked workers safely. The lifecycle smoke passes under ASan/UBSan and
ThreadSanitizer with the publication protocol, and the Pi matrix was rerun
with it enabled.

Feasibility decision
--------------------

1. Multiple serial NAM blocks are computationally feasible as a bounded graph
   feature. One NAM plus cab is comfortable. Two NAM lanes plus one shared cab
   are now zero-miss feasible in the 64-frame sustained model screen when the
   explicit CPU1/CPU0/CPU3 contract is used; three NAM plus cab is not yet
   admitted.

2. Two or three NAM lanes can run concurrently in terms of total CPU work, but
   direct “submit this block and spin/wait until every worker finishes” is not a
   production-safe 64-frame execution contract. Its p99.9 crosses the deadline
   even without a cab, and the cab cases miss repeatedly.

3. A shared post-mix cab on the callback is not a solution: it serializes the
   convolver onto the critical path. The dedicated post-stage worker remains
   the right bounded-latency architecture. The worker-published handoff removes
   one callback copy and improves the short/mean measurements, but the
   corrected CPU2 runs still show a scheduler tail above the 64-frame budget,
   while the CPU1 placement screen passes. Admission therefore needs explicit
   one-block latency, bounded slots, bypass/hold fallback, a measured
   worker/core policy, and a sustained tail margin tied to that placement.

4. The first implementation should therefore be a bounded, preallocated graph:
   serial pre-chain -> fixed lane group -> serial/post stage. Do not expose an
   arbitrary DAG or unbounded number of workers. The graph compiler must reject
   or downgrade plans whose measured p99.9 plus safety margin exceeds the
   selected block budget.

5. For the tested two-lane topology, the current candidate core policy is audio
   CPU1, lane worker CPU0, and post worker CPU3. CPU2 remains the shipped
   service assignment until a deployment-level change is approved. CPU0 should
   otherwise remain housekeeping unless the image explicitly isolates it.
   Persistent workers, fixed priorities/affinity, no per-block allocation, and
   an underflow/over-budget telemetry counter are required.

Required next DSP experiment before UI/layout work
---------------------------------------------------

The next DSP slice should extend the private DSP path (still no UI):

* use the new per-lane/post-stage process-time, handoff-wait, publication-ring,
  underflow, and worker-CPU telemetry in admission reports;
* repeat the 32/64/128-frame matrix and 60-second run after any scheduler or
  ring policy change, including live-service and thermal conditions; validate
  the CPU1/CPU0/CPU3 contract during the deployment/configuration step;
* define a numerical headroom/zero-miss admission threshold from those tails;
* keep the lifecycle smoke in the gate while adding larger real-model swaps.

Only after that scheduler passes a zero-fault sustained run with a defined
headroom policy should the preset schema and UI layout be designed.
