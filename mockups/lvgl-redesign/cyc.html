Direction E — Cyc

A preset is a lighting cue, and the screen is the cyclorama behind the player. Only the running cue is lit — it holds a large flat field of its own colour; the other three sit in blackout with a single hairline. A real cyc is a smooth wash, which is exactly what the panel cannot render, so this one is built the way a cyc looks broken into its individual fixtures: discrete bands, hard edges, no blend.

The room comes up as you get closer. Far out of tune, one fixture is lit on a black horizon. As you approach pitch the neighbouring fixtures warm, and on landing inside the outlined zone the whole horizon goes to white day. It is the cyclorama's dawn sequence used as a tuning readout, and every state is a flat colour step rather than a fade.

What this costs in LVGL

  • Cheap, with one caveat. Large flat colour fields are a single rect fill — trivial. But a full-width field redrawn every frame is the biggest blit in any of these directions; keep it on the background layer and never invalidate it for a value-only update. The existing revision-counter architecture already makes that easy.
  • Discrete step bars (master fader, tuner horizon, parameter levels) are 10–22 small rects each. Cheap, but build them as a reusable widget rather than rebuilding children on every change — flip a style on existing objects instead.
  • Fonts: Oswald at four sizes plus Martian Mono at three. Martian Mono is wide, so budget more glyph bitmap area than a normal mono; subset it to digits, units and a few caps.
  • The tuner's dawn sequence is the one real piece of new logic: mapping cents deviation to a discrete fixture ramp with a named state per step. That is a small pure function and easy to unit-test — it belongs next to the existing tuner presentation code, not in the view.
  • Honest risk: the live cue's colour has to come from somewhere. Either the player picks it per preset (a real feature, and the settings screen already offers a global accent), or it is derived from slot index. Deriving it is free but makes two banks look alike; letting the player choose is better and costs one field in the preset schema.
