Direction C — Sheet

The pedal shows you the schematic. Ardor's position is that it is open and inspectable, so the interface becomes the drawing: reference designators, net labels, dimension lines, a title block, revision triangles. Everything is linework on a flat ink field — hairlines and hard edges only, so there is nothing for RGB565 to quantise.

What this costs in LVGL

  • Linework is the expense. The schematic screens are drawn here in SVG; LVGL has no SVG renderer you want in an audio-realtime process. Rebuild them as lv_line point arrays — accurate and cheap, since every path in this design is orthogonal or a single diagonal. Budget one point array per net, rebuilt only on topology change (the existing revision-counter architecture already does this).
  • The drafting grid must be a tiled 40×40 image, not 60-odd line objects. One RGB565 tile, ~3 KB, drawn by the background layer.
  • Hatching (bypassed blocks, tolerance band, fault sheets) is the same trick: a 12×12 diagonal tile. Never generate hatch lines as objects.
  • Two font families at many sizes is the heaviest font bill of the three directions — Barlow Condensed at 5 sizes plus Chivo Mono at 4. Subset Chivo Mono to digits, unit letters and punctuation and it stays modest; the 196 px tuner glyph set is 12 characters.
  • Genuine stretch: the miniature per-preset schematics on screen 1 must be generated from real preset data at load, not hand-drawn. That is a new layout routine — roughly what LvglChainLayout already does, at one-quarter scale and without interaction.
