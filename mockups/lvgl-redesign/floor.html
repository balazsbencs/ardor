Routing is taped, not disguised. The split is two physically separate lanes of tape on the floor with stencilled L and R, the way a stage is actually marked out. Bypass is a marker X — the universal gesture for "this one's out." Nothing here is a rounded card with a subtle border.

What this costs in LVGL

  • Free. Every surface is a flat lv_obj with a solid background — cheaper to render than the current gradient panels, and immune to RGB565 banding by construction.
  • Fonts: the real cost. Anton at 26/40/52/70/280 px must each be compiled as a separate LVGL bitmap font. The 280 px tuner face is glyph-limited (12 note letters, sharp sign) so subset it aggressively — a full ASCII set at that size would be several hundred KB of flash.
  • Torn tape edges are clip-path here. LVGL has no clip-path: ship them as small 9-patch or tiled PNGs, or accept clean rectangles (the design survives it — the hierarchy is doing the work, not the torn edge).
  • Hazard diagonals on the fault row need a small tiled image; a repeating 32×32 RGB565 tile costs 2 KB and draws fast.
  • Marker strike-through on bypassed blocks: two rotated rects. Use lv_line with thick line width instead of rotating an object — cheaper and exact.
