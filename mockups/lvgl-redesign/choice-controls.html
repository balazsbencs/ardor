Many-value choice
parameters

Ten working ways to pick one of nineteen values with a fingertip, on a dark stage, at a steep angle. Every study below is interactive: tap, drag, spin, or press and hold. All geometry is real — 1280 × 720 screen, 385 × 132 parameter cells, the same Slate palette and Saira nomenclature as the shipping panel.

What is broken today

A discrete parameter renders as one segmented row inside the cell. The row is kTravelWidth = 337 px and splits evenly across the option count (LvglUiParameterRenderer.cpp:632-652). The font already drops to 11 px above three options — that is the tell. Segment width per parameter as shipped:

A fingertip contact patch is 45–57 px wide. Below four options the row is fine; above it, the control is decoration.

00The incumbent, to scale

Whammy’s parameter page exactly as it renders now. The Preset row is real: 19 segments of 17 px carrying 11 px type. Try to hit “Dive bomb” on the first attempt.

BaselineUnusable above 4

01Stepper + grid picker

The cell keeps the value large and gains two 72 px nudge pads for neighbour steps — the common musical move. Tapping the value opens the full panel as a grid of 228 × 76 px tiles, grouped by the families the catalog already records. One tap commits and closes. Nineteen options fit without a single scroll.

RecommendedFewest new parts

02Expand in place

No overlay, no new layer. Tapping a choice cell swaps the six-cell grid for the option grid in the same rectangle, with a back affordance where the page pager sits. The parameter page and the picker are the same surface at two zoom levels. Keeping the panel frame costs height: tiles land at 62 px instead of 76 px.

CalmestNo modal layer

03Detented drum

The cell holds a three-row drum behind a lamp-coloured detent gate. Flick it, and it spins and settles on a value. It is the touch twin of the rotary encoder — the same mental model under both hands — and it costs no screen outside the cell.

Encoder twinCell-local

04Family tray

Nineteen presets are really ten Whammy modes plus nine Harmony modes. Tap the cell and a tray opens across the panel width: family on the left rail, members across the full 1030 px. Every member gets ~100 px because the tray borrows the width the cell never had.

Truest to dataTwo taps, always

05Radial dial

The options wrap into a ring around a hub that reads the current value. It matches the one physical control the pedal already has — the rotary encoder — so the screen and the knob describe the same shape. Tap a spoke, or press and drag around the ring and release.

Most physicalHardware rhyme

06Filmstrip gate

A horizontal strip runs behind a fixed gate. Drag it left or right and it snaps, one option per detent; the option under the gate is the value. Neighbours stay visible on both sides, so the player can see what the next stomp-adjacent value is without opening anything.

Best contextNeighbours visible

07Instrument map

Stop treating musical values as a list. Harmonizer Key is twelve semitones, so it is drawn as a keyboard. Interval is a signed distance, so it is drawn as a ladder running down through unison to up. The layout carries the meaning; no player has to read every label to find the one they want.

Most nativePer-parameter work

08Thumb marking menu

Press and hold the cell. The options bloom around the finger in two rings, follow the drag, and commit on release — one continuous gesture, no round trip, no lifting the hand. Fastest once learnt. It also shows its own limit: nineteen wedges crowd, and the menu must shift to stay on screen. Press and hold the Preset cell to try it.

Fastest expertCrowds past ~12

09Side sheet list

The block drawer already solves “pick one of thirty-five” with a 480 px sheet of 72 px rows and sticky group headers. This reuses it verbatim for parameters. Largest targets of any concept, one familiar pattern for both jobs, and the least new code in production — at the cost of covering a third of the screen.

Cheapest buildReuses the drawer

10Recents, then everything

A player uses three presets in a set, not nineteen. The cell keeps the three most recent values as full-size tiles plus an ALL pad to the complete grid. The common case is one tap and never opens a picker; the rare case is two.

Fewest tapsNeeds stored state
