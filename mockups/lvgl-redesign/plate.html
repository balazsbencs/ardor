Direction B — Plate

One bit. Black and white pixels only, with Bayer-ordered stipple standing in for every gray. Dithering is not a workaround here, it is the design language — and it is the textbook answer to a panel that cannot hold a smooth ramp. Effect families are carried by pattern instead of hue, so the system stays legible with the colour channel removed entirely.

What this costs in LVGL

  • Cheapest of the three, by a distance. Two colours means the framebuffer compresses trivially and every fill is a solid rect. Banding is not mitigated here, it is impossible.
  • Dither patterns ship as 4×4 and 2×2 tiled images — under 100 bytes each, drawn with lv_image tiling or a custom lv_draw pattern fill. Six family patterns plus four gray levels is well under 1 KB total.
  • Marching ants need a repeating 8px dash image scrolled by a timer, or four lv_line objects with a dashed style and an animated offset. Budget one 20 Hz timer; it only runs while something is selected. This is the one genuine stretch — LVGL has no native marching-ants primitive.
  • Bitmap fonts are free wins here. Silkscreen and Pixelify are already pixel grids, so compiling them at integer sizes gives exact, hint-free rendering — no antialiasing artefacts on a 16-bit panel, and 1bpp glyph storage instead of 4bpp cuts font flash by 4×.
  • The real cost is not technical. You lose hue as an information channel. The design answers that with pattern, but it is a genuine trade and worth deciding deliberately.
