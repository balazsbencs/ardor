Direction F — Sleeve

The quietest one. Hierarchy carried entirely by scale and empty space — the running preset is simply set four times larger than the others, and that is the whole mechanism. There is not one card, button outline or filled panel in the system. Lowercase throughout, which no modeler on the market does, one off-centre rule the layout hangs from, and a single muted blue used exactly once per screen.

What this costs in LVGL

  • The cheapest to render and the cheapest to maintain. Labels, 1px rules, a handful of small rects. No panels, no borders, no images, no patterns. This would measurably reduce UI draw time against the current build.
  • Fonts are the entire budget, and it is a real one. Jost at 200/300/400 across roughly six sizes, including a 200 px lowercase face for the tuner. Light weights at large sizes have thin stems, and thin stems on a 16-bit panel with LVGL's 4bpp antialiasing can look ragged — this is the one direction where you should compile a test font and look at it on the actual display before committing.
  • Contrast needs checking on hardware, not in the simulator. The grey secondary text sits around 7:1 on paper, but a dark stage plus a steep viewing angle plus panel gamma is a different story. Budget one session with the real panel to tune the three grey values.
  • Touch targets are invisible. Text-only controls with no boxes still need 44 px hit areas — LVGL makes that easy with lv_obj_set_ext_click_area, but it must be deliberate or the design becomes unusable with a boot on.
  • Honest risk: this is the direction most likely to read as "unfinished" to someone expecting a modeler UI, and the one most dependent on typography being exactly right. It has the highest ceiling and the least margin for sloppy execution.
