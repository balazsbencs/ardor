When a block leaves the signal path

Five quieter ways to make bypass unmistakable without spending Ardor’s live red. Every study keeps the effect name readable, preserves its place in the chain, and uses shape or depth so color is never the only cue.

One semantic correction Red means running or selected. Bypass should read as absence, disconnection, or direct passage—not alarm.
ChorusBypass

Current treatment. The red edge and status badge compete for attention even though this block is doing less. The studies below replace both.

1Recommended

Dry rail

A literal signal channel crosses the faceplate. The effect stays identifiable above it, while two jack points and a continuous rail explain that audio passes through untouched.

Best fit: clearest link to Ardor’s signal-flow language; needs a little vertical room.

Recessed plate

The whole block drops behind the working surface. A large engraved “Off” replaces the badge; the effect name remains present but quiet, like an unpowered rack unit.

Best fit: calmest treatment and very easy to implement; depth alone may be subtle on poor displays.

Open circuit

A familiar switch diagram becomes the state marker. It is compact, technical without being decorative, and remains unambiguous in monochrome or high-contrast viewing.

Best fit: strongest non-verbal cue; some players may read the open circuit as “no audio” rather than dry-through.

Outline frame

The filled faceplate disappears, leaving a dashed footprint and a small universal “not active” mark. The layout stays stable while the visual weight falls away.

Best fit: economical and easy to scan; dashed edges are less native to the existing hard-rule system.

Folded block

The inactive processor collapses to a labeled spacer. Long chains become easier to inspect, yet the block keeps its order and can be restored from the same switch.

Best fit: maximum density and clearest active/inactive distinction; the width change can make chain editing feel less stable.
